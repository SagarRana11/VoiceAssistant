# Unstable Angina Procedure Guidance

## Goal
Identify blocked heart arteries quickly and prevent progression to heart attack.

## Before Procedure
- ECG and symptom assessment
- Blood tests
- IV line inserted
- Consent completed
- Monitoring started
- Allergy and kidney history checked

## During Procedure
1. Local anesthesia at wrist or groin
2. Catheter inserted into artery
3. Guided to heart arteries using imaging
4. Contrast dye used to locate blockage
5. Balloon or stent used if required
6. Blood flow rechecked

## Equipment Used
- Catheter
- Guidewire
- Balloon
- Stent
- Imaging system
- ECG monitor

## Typical Duration
30 minutes to 2 hours.

## Will It Hurt
Usually mild discomfort only.

## After Procedure
- Observation in CCU / ward
- Monitor puncture site
- Continue medicines
- Repeat ECG if needed

## Possible Complications
- Bleeding
- Bruising
- Chest pain
- Arrhythmia
- Contrast reaction

## Red Flags After Procedure
- New chest pain
- Heavy bleeding
- Severe swelling
- Breathlessness

## FAQ

Q: Is stent always needed?  
A: No, only if significant blockage is found.

Q: Will I stay in hospital?  
A: Usually yes for monitoring.

## Voice Short Response
Doctors check your heart arteries and may treat blockage if needed.
