# STEMI Procedure Guidance

## Goal

Restore blood flow to the blocked heart artery as quickly as possible.

## Before Procedure

- ECG and emergency evaluation
- Blood tests
- IV line inserted
- Consent completed
- Allergy and kidney history checked
- Monitoring started

## During Procedure

1. Local anesthesia given at wrist or groin
2. Catheter inserted into artery
3. Guided to heart arteries using imaging
4. Dye injected to locate blockage
5. Balloon used to open blockage if needed
6. Stent may be placed
7. Blood flow rechecked

## Equipment Used

- Catheter
- Guidewire
- Balloon
- Stent
- X-ray imaging system
- ECG monitor

## Typical Duration

30 minutes to 2 hours depending on complexity.

## Will It Hurt

Usually only mild discomfort. Local anesthesia is used.

## After Procedure

- Shift to ICU / CCU
- Monitoring heart rhythm
- Check puncture site
- Continue medicines
- Gradual movement after doctor advice

## Possible Complications

- Bleeding
- Swelling
- Chest pain
- Irregular heartbeat
- Contrast reaction
- Rare emergency surgery

## Red Flags After Procedure

- New chest pain
- Severe swelling
- Continuous bleeding
- Breathlessness
- Fainting

## FAQ

Q: Will I be awake?  
A: Usually yes, but medicines may help you relax.

Q: Can more than one stent be needed?  
A: Yes, depending on blockage pattern.

## Voice Short Response

Doctors use a thin tube to open the blocked heart artery and improve blood flow.
