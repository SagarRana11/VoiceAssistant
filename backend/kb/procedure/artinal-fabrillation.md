# Atrial Fibrillation Procedure Guidance

## Goal
Restore normal rhythm or improve control of irregular heartbeat.

## Before Procedure
- ECG confirmation
- Blood tests
- Anticoagulation review
- Consent completed
- Fasting if sedation needed

## Possible Procedures
- Electrical cardioversion
- Catheter ablation

## During Cardioversion
1. Sedation given
2. Controlled electrical shock delivered
3. Rhythm checked

## During Ablation
1. Catheters inserted through groin veins
2. Guided to heart
3. Abnormal rhythm areas treated
4. Rhythm monitored

## Equipment Used
- ECG monitor
- Defibrillator
- Catheters
- Mapping system
- Imaging tools

## Typical Duration
Cardioversion: 20–30 minutes  
Ablation: 2–4 hours

## Will It Hurt
Sedation is used. Mild soreness may occur later.

## After Procedure
- Rhythm monitoring
- Continue blood thinners as advised
- Follow-up ECG

## Possible Complications
- Bleeding
- Rhythm recurrence
- Stroke (rare)
- Heart injury (rare)

## Red Flags After Procedure
- Chest pain
- Fainting
- Severe palpitations
- Breathlessness

## FAQ

Q: Will rhythm stay normal forever?  
A: Sometimes yes, but recurrence can happen.

Q: Need medicines after procedure?  
A: Often yes, at least initially.

## Voice Short Response
This procedure helps restore or control your heart rhythm.
