# Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)

## Full Form
Coronary Artery Disease

## Clinical Meaning
CAD is a condition where the heart arteries become narrowed or blocked by cholesterol plaque, reducing blood flow to the heart.

## Patient Explanation
Fatty deposits have built up inside your heart arteries. This makes it harder for blood to reach the heart muscle and can cause chest pain or heart attack.

## Why It Is Serious
- Can cause angina
- Can lead to heart attack
- Can weaken heart function
- May worsen silently over time

## Common Symptoms
- Chest pain on walking or exertion
- Breathlessness
- Fatigue
- Reduced exercise capacity
- Sometimes no symptoms

## Risk Factors
- Smoking
- High cholesterol
- Diabetes
- High blood pressure
- Obesity
- Low activity
- Family history

## Diagnosis
- ECG
- Stress test
- CT coronary scan
- Coronary angiography
- Lipid profile
- Echocardiography

## Treatment
- Lifestyle improvement
- Aspirin in selected patients
- Statins
- Blood pressure control
- Angioplasty / stent
- Bypass surgery if severe

## Risks If Delayed
- Heart attack
- Progressive blockage
- Heart failure
- Sudden cardiac events

## Recovery Outlook
CAD can be managed successfully with long-term treatment and healthy habits.

## FAQ

Q: Can blockage be removed naturally?  
A: Lifestyle helps, but medical treatment is usually needed.

Q: Do all CAD patients need stent?  
A: No, treatment depends on severity.

Q: Can exercise help?  
A: Yes, when guided safely.

## Voice Short Response
Your heart arteries are narrowed by plaque. Treatment improves blood flow and protects your heart.
