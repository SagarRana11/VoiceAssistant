# Heart Failure

## Full Form
Heart Failure

## Clinical Meaning
Heart failure is a condition where the heart cannot pump blood effectively enough to meet the body’s needs.

## Patient Explanation
Your heart has become weaker or stiffer, so it cannot circulate blood properly. This can cause fluid buildup and tiredness.

## Why It Is Serious
- Can cause fluid in lungs
- Can reduce oxygen delivery
- Can worsen kidneys
- May need repeated hospitalization

## Common Symptoms
- Breathlessness
- Swelling in legs
- Tiredness
- Fast heartbeat
- Difficulty lying flat
- Sudden weight gain

## Risk Factors
- Heart attack history
- High blood pressure
- Diabetes
- Valve disease
- Alcohol misuse
- Cardiomyopathy

## Diagnosis
- Echocardiography
- BNP blood test
- ECG
- Chest X-ray
- Blood tests

## Treatment
- Diuretics
- ACE inhibitors / ARB / ARNI
- Beta blockers
- SGLT2 medicines
- Low salt diet
- Devices in some patients

## Risks If Delayed
- Severe breathlessness
- Fluid overload
- Kidney damage
- Shock
- Death

## Recovery Outlook
Many patients improve significantly with medicines, monitoring, and follow-up care.

## FAQ

Q: Is heart failure permanent?  
A: It can often be managed and sometimes improved.

Q: Why daily weight check?  
A: Sudden gain may mean fluid buildup.

Q: Can I walk?  
A: Yes, gradual exercise is helpful if approved.

## Voice Short Response
Your heart is weaker than normal, but treatment can help you breathe better and live better.
