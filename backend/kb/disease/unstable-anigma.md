# Unstable Angina

## Full Form
Unstable Angina

## Clinical Meaning
Unstable angina is a serious heart condition where reduced blood flow to the heart causes chest pain at rest or with very little activity. It is part of acute coronary syndrome and may lead to a heart attack if untreated.

## Patient Explanation
Your heart arteries have become narrowed or irritated, and the heart is not getting enough oxygen-rich blood. This causes warning chest pain and means urgent treatment is needed.

## Why It Is Serious
- May progress to heart attack
- Pain can occur even at rest
- Indicates unstable artery blockage
- Risk of dangerous rhythm problems
- Requires urgent medical attention

## Common Symptoms
- Chest pressure or tightness
- Pain spreading to arm, jaw, back
- Sweating
- Breathlessness
- Nausea
- Anxiety
- Pain not relieved by rest

## Risk Factors
- Smoking
- High cholesterol
- Diabetes
- High blood pressure
- Obesity
- Stress
- Family history of heart disease

## Diagnosis
- ECG
- Troponin blood tests
- Clinical symptoms review
- Echocardiography
- Coronary angiography

## Treatment
- Hospital admission
- Aspirin and blood thinners
- Nitroglycerin
- Statins
- Beta blockers
- Angiography if required
- Angioplasty / stent if blockage found

## Risks If Delayed
- Heart attack
- Sudden worsening chest pain
- Heart failure
- Dangerous arrhythmia
- Sudden death

## Recovery Outlook
Many patients recover well when treated early and follow long-term heart care.

## FAQ

Q: Is unstable angina a heart attack?  
A: No, but it is a warning sign and can become one.

Q: Should I ignore pain if it improves?  
A: No. Immediate medical review is needed.

Q: Can I live normally after treatment?  
A: Yes, with medicines and lifestyle changes.

## Voice Short Response
You have warning chest pain because the heart is not getting enough blood. Fast treatment helps prevent a heart attack.
